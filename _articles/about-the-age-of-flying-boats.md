Today, *Porco Rosso* will be broadcast on Friday Roadshow. This film is a blockbuster theatrical anime directed by Hayao Miyazaki that was released in 1992, but did you know that it’s based on an original work?

At the time, Director Miyazaki had been churning out feature-length films one after another—*Nausicaä of the Valley of the Wind* (1984), *Castle in the Sky* (1985), *My Neighbor Totoro* (1988), and *Kiki’s Delivery Service* (1989)—but this pace proved too grueling, and he was completely exhausted.

However, Studio Ghibli had just made its staff full-time employees, and in order to pay their salaries, they had to keep producing new works without a break.

That’s when the idea emerged: “Let’s make a short film instead of a feature-length one.”

Around that time, Miyazaki was serializing a manga called “Random Thoughts Notebook” in the model-making magazine *Monthly Model Graphics*, so the team thought that an episode titled “The Age of Flying Boats” might be suitable for adaptation into a film.

Furthermore, producer Toshio Suzuki suggested, “Since it’s a story about airplanes, why not ask an airline for cooperation?” As a result, the project moved forward as a “short film to be screened on Japan Airlines (JAL) flights.”

However, if you read the notes in *Random Thoughts Notebook*, it states: “The plan for a film adaptation arose around the time I was writing the first installment of ‘The Flying Boat Era,’ and the second and third installments served as ‘storyboards’ for that presentation.”

Originally, *Random Thoughts Notebook* was a short manga series where each story was wrapped up in five pages, and *The Era of Flying Boats* was also supposed to end in a single installment, but it seems he drew the continuation because the film adaptation project got underway… (The following is from Director Miyazaki’s comments).

“The Age of Flying Boats,” well, to put it simply, I should’ve just ended it after the first episode, you know! I was such an idiot—I just went ahead and drew the rest, so in a way, it ended up feeling like I couldn’t bring it to a close anymore, emotionally speaking.

(From *Hayao Miyazaki’s Notebook of Random Thoughts*)

In other words, Director Miyazaki probably didn’t intend for it to be such a long story, but then what exactly is the story of the first episode of *The Age of Flying Boats*?

The Age of Flying Boats

The Age of Flying Boats

When you read it, it starts with a scene where the Mamma Yuto air pirates attack a ship, steal money and valuables, and take a young girl hostage before fleeing.

Next, the protagonist, Buta (Lieutenant Marco Pagot), is relaxing at his hideout when the phone rings, and he takes off into the sky in his beloved Savoia S-21.

After gathering intelligence on the Mamma Yuto Gang from the attacked ship, he spots their seaplane while passing a sightseeing boat—where young women call out to him, “Mr. Pig!”—along the way.

The enemy is easily sunk by the Savoia S-21’s attack, the kidnapped girl is safely rescued, and all’s well that ends well… that’s the gist of it.

In short, it’s almost identical to the opening scene of *Porco Rosso*; the only real difference is that “only one girl was kidnapped” (in the movie, more than ten were kidnapped).

Naturally, the storyboards for the movie were also drawn based on this original work, but when Producer Suzuki was handed the storyboards with the words, “It’s finished—take a look!” he was absolutely stunned.

Since it was based on the first chapter, the story ended right at the scene where the protagonist rescues the children from the Mamma Yuto Gang. Without thinking, Mr. Suzuki blurted out, “Huh? Is that it!?”

The opening scene of *Porco Rosso* (up to the point where the children are rescued) is only about 10 minutes long, so even for a short film, this was far too short.
